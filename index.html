About this manual

This is not a real workshop manual and nothing in it should be used to fix a car. It is a record of every car I have owned, written up in the style of the manuals that lived in the glovebox of most of them. Factory figures are for the model as built and are given from memory and published sources; where a detail is uncertain it is marked to be confirmed. Owner's notes are mine and are not to be taken as engineering advice.

Chapter1

Fiat 126The first car · 594 / 652 cc air-cooled twin · rear engine

Registrationnot recordedOwned c. 1990 tbc
▲ Top

General description

The 126 replaced the Fiat 500 in 1972 and kept its layout: a two-cylinder, air-cooled engine slung out behind the rear axle, driving the rear wheels through a four-speed gearbox with no synchromesh on first. Early cars had 594 cc and 23 bhp; from 1977 the engine grew to 652 cc, and from 1987 the Polish-built 126 BIS gained a 704 cc water-cooled unit laid flat under the boot floor. UK cars were right-hand drive, imported in modest numbers and, by the time this one was bought, cheap.

It is a very small car. Four people fit, provided they are friends. The heater takes warm air from the engine cooling fan, which means it works only when the engine is working hard, and smells faintly of the engine bay.

Ford FiestaMk3 · N-registration · red

RegistrationN ··· ···First registered 1995/96 · owned tbc
▲ Top

General description

The fourth-generation Fiesta went on sale in October 1995, at the start of the N-registration year, replacing the boxy Mk3 (which limped on as the "Fiesta Classic" until 1997). The Mk4 brought the new Zetec-SE 1.25 engine, a much better interior and, for the first time in a small Ford, a car that was pleasant to steer. It was Britain's best-selling car for most of the late nineties.

The base engines were the 1.3 Endura-E pushrod unit (60 bhp) carried over from the Mk3, and the 1.25 Zetec-SE (75 bhp). A 1.4 Zetec-SE and a 1.8 diesel were also offered.

Identification

An N-plate red Fiesta could be either a late Mk3 "Classic" (slotted grille, flat bonnet) or an early Mk4 (oval grille, oval lamps). The photo chosen for the drawing is a Mk3, so that is what is shown. Confirm the engine (1.1 / 1.3 / 1.4 / 1.8D).

Toyota MR2 TurboMk2 (SW20) · mid-engine · T-bar roof · red · modified

Registrationnot recordedOwned 1990s tbc
▲ Top

General description

The second-generation MR2 arrived in 1989 as a bigger, softer-looking car than the wedge-shaped original, with a mid-mounted transverse engine, rear-wheel drive and, in Turbo form, the 2.0-litre 3S-GTE from the Celica GT-Four. Toyota GB never officially sold the Turbo, so almost every one in Britain is a Japanese-market import. Early "Revision 1" cars had a reputation for snap oversteer, which Toyota addressed from 1992 with wider rear tyres and revised suspension geometry — a fix that a set of even wider rear tyres and lowered springs will cheerfully undo.

The T-bar roof, standard on most imports, gives two lift-out glass panels that stow behind the seats.

Saab 900Classic (pre-GM) · 2.0 16-valve, non-turbo · white

RegistrationK140 MEUFirst registered 1992/93 · owned tbc
▲ Top

General description

The 900 was Saab's aeroplane-company idea of a car, built from 1978 to 1993 on an evolution of the 99. The deeply curved windscreen and forward-hinged clamshell bonnet came from a company that also made jets, as did the ignition key between the seats, the heated seats, the headlamp wipers and the obsessive crash structure. The engine — a slant-four derived from the old Triumph unit — sits back-to-front with the gearbox underneath it, driving the front wheels.

By the K-registration year the classic 900 was in its last months; the GM-based "new generation" 900 replaced it in late 1993. Non-turbo 16-valve cars (the 900i 16) had 128 bhp and, with the long-throw five-speed, a distinctive throaty note.

Saab 9000 AeroCS body · turbo · deep blue · heated leather bucket seats

RegistrationM218 VCHFirst registered 1994/95 · owned tbc
▲ Top

General description

The 9000 was Saab's big car, sharing its platform with the Fiat Croma, Lancia Thema and Alfa 164 but sharing almost nothing you could see. The CS hatchback body of 1992 tidied the nose and tail; the Aero of 1993 added the full-pressure 2.3 turbo, a five-speed box, sports suspension, a body kit and the seats — big leather sports chairs with heating and inflatable side bolsters that were, for many years, the best in any car.

The UK Aero was sold with the 2.3-litre B234R engine at 225 bhp (200 with the automatic). A 2.0-litre turbo Aero was built for Italy and other tax-sensitive markets.

Identification

Remembered as a 2.0-litre. UK-market Aeros were 2.3-litre; a 2.0 turbo would normally be a CS/CSE, or an imported Italian-spec Aero. Confirm 2.0 or 2.3, manual or automatic. Specifications below are for the 2.3 Aero, with the 2.0 turbo figure noted.

Fiat 500 Sport0.9 TwinAir · 875 cc two-cylinder turbo · white, black interior

RegistrationCE10 XVWFirst registered 2010 (Cardiff) · owned c. 2010–2013

General description

The 2007 500 revived the 1957 shape on the Panda platform, built in Tychy, Poland — the same factory that turned out the 126 for two decades. In 2010 Fiat fitted it with the TwinAir: a two-cylinder, 875 cc turbocharged engine with hydraulically operated inlet valves (MultiAir) that made 85 bhp, weighed almost nothing and sounded like a cheerful lawnmower. Fiat quoted 68 mpg, which nobody has ever seen. It won International Engine of the Year in 2011.

The Sport added 16-inch alloys, a rear spoiler, side skirts, sports seats and a leather steering wheel.

Volkswagen T25 CamperWater-cooled · wedge pop-top · yellow & white · concurrent vehicle

RegistrationE431 DODFirst registered 1987/88 · owned alongside Ch. 6 & 8, later sold
▲ Top

General description

The third-generation Transporter (T25 in Britain, T3 elsewhere) ran from 1979 to 1992, the last VW van with the engine in the back. From 1982 the air-cooled flat-four gave way to the water-cooled "wasserboxer" — the same layout, now with coolant, a radiator at the front and a long run of pipework in between. The 1.9 (DG) gave 78 bhp; the 2.1 injection (DJ) 112 bhp. Either way the van is slow, the driver sits over the front wheels with the pedals ahead of the axle, and the noise comes from behind.

An elevating wedge roof (Devon, Autohomes and others) hinges at the front and lifts at the rear, giving standing height for cooking and, in the better ones, a bunk in the roof.

Alfa's supermini, built from 2008 to 2018 in Turin on the Grande Punto platform and styled after the 8C Competizione. The TwinAir in MiTo form was tuned to 105 bhp and paired with a six-speed gearbox; Alfa's DNA switch changes throttle, steering weight and stability control between Dynamic, Normal and All-weather. RegistrationFH69 ZGOFirst registered late 2019 · owned 2019/20 – present

General description

The second-generation Tiguan (2016–2024) sits on the MQB platform with the Golf and everything else; the R-Line trim adds the sporting body kit, big wheels and sports seats, and this one has the full equipment list on top. 4Motion is a Haldex-type clutch pack that sends drive to the rear wheels when the front ones need help. The 2.0 TDI was offered at 150 PS and 190 PS; the latter came only with the seven-speed DSG.

After nine cars whose luxuries ran to heated seats and a wedge roof, this one has heated seats all round, a head-up display, adaptive cruise, park assist and the electrics to run a small house.
